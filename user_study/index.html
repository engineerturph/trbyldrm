Perceptual Study on Talking-Face Video Generation

Anonymous research questionnaire · about 8 minutes

Thank you for taking part. In this study you will watch 12 short videos (15 seconds each). Every video shows the same person, animated from the same photo and the same speech recording by two different computer methods, placed side by side as A (left) and B (right). Which method is on which side changes from video to video.

What to do

  1. Turn the sound on (headphones are best) and watch each video to the end. You may replay it as often as you like.
  2. The first video is a practice round: it explains the questions and is not counted.
  3. Answer five questions for each video by choosing A, B or Draw (no real difference):
    • Lip sync — which one has the better lip sync?
    • Non-verbal alignment — which one looks more natural?
    • Motion richness — which one moves in a richer, more varied way?
    • Reactiveness — which one reacts more to what is being said?
    • Overall preference — which one would you prefer to talk to?
  4. There are no right or wrong answers; go with your own impression.

A laptop or desktop screen works best; on a phone, please hold it sideways.

Privacy

The study is anonymous. We store only your answers, how long each video took, and a random code made by your browser so that your 12 answers stay together. No name, e-mail or address is collected. The answers are used for academic research only. Taking part is voluntary and you may stop at any time by closing the page.

Video 1 of 12
Practice round. This example works exactly like the 12 videos that follow, but its answers are not recorded. Use it to get used to the five questions:
  • Lip sync — watch the mouth: do the lips open, close and shape the sounds at the right moments?
  • Non-verbal alignment — watch the whole face: do the expressions, the eyes and the head movement look natural for a person saying this?
  • Motion richness — is the movement lively and varied, or stiff and repetitive?
  • Reactiveness — does the face follow the speech: stressing words, pausing, changing with the tone of voice?
  • Overall preference — all things considered, which one would you prefer to talk to?
